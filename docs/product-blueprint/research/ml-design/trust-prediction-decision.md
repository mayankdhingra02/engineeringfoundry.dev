# Trust, prediction, forecasting, and decision systems synthesis

Reviewed: 2026-09-04  
Scope: fraud, trust and safety, ETA, and demand-forecasting dossiers.

## Accepted decision claims

- Predictions become product decisions through thresholds, policies, queues, capacity, and human escalation; those layers must be designed together.
- False-positive and false-negative costs are asymmetric and change by slice, operating point, and intervention.
- Delayed or censored labels, adversarial adaptation, temporal leakage, and intervention effects must be addressed in data and evaluation plans.
- Forecasts require a horizon, baseline, time-aware validation, uncertainty treatment, and a downstream decision rule.
- High-impact controls require auditability, appeal or escalation paths, monitoring, and explicit rollback authority.

## Evidence and mapping

`SRC-ML-METRICS`, `SRC-ML-TFDV`, `SRC-ML-TECH-DEBT`, and `SRC-ML-FORECASTING` support measurement and temporal reasoning. `SRC-ML-NIST-RMF`, `SRC-ML-TEST-SCORE`, `SRC-ML-EXPERIMENT-PRE`, and `SRC-ML-GOOGLE-SLO` support risk, validation, and operating controls.

## Exclusions

The synthesis does not assign a universal threshold, fairness definition, forecast metric, or automated enforcement policy.
