# Mock prescription and returned evidence

## Boundary

The Playbook decides whether simulation is useful, defines its objective and target, and consumes normalized evidence. The Mock Lab owns session setup, prompts, interviewer behavior, timers, interruptions, reflection, evaluator input, and optional recording controls.

## Prescription

A prescription identifies objective, target round, scope, fidelity, evaluator, duration basis, environment, permitted references, hint/interruption policy, company/role/level modifiers, evidence dimensions, prior-exposure exclusions, and accessibility settings. A generic simulation must remain useful when company facts are unknown.

## Evidence return

Returned evidence keeps deterministic events, human observation, AI-assisted observation, and self-report visibly separate. It carries session/content fingerprint, conditions, evaluator provenance, fresh or repeated status, validity, hints, interruptions, technical failure, dimension confidence, and remediation tags. One mock never becomes ground truth or a hiring prediction.

The current integration uses saved mock self-reviews only as labeled self-report. Peer matching, scheduling, moderation, recording, and advanced evaluation remain Mock Lab work, not hidden Playbook scope.
