# Community and challenge model

Engineering Foundry currently provides an original, self-guided Engineering Challenge Lab and a public Community Hub. Both remain useful without authentication, database persistence, or hosted Supabase configuration.

## Current Challenge Lab

The registry contains eight original challenges across DSA, System Design, ML System Design, and Backend Engineering. Every challenge defines a prompt, context, deliverables, constraints, success criteria, a suggested practice time and workflow, revealable considerations, common mistakes, stretch goals, and a track-specific qualitative rubric.

Challenge levels—Foundation, Intermediate, and Advanced—are Engineering Foundry preparation labels. They do not map to employer titles or interview levels. Guidance offers directions, tradeoffs, and questions to test; it is not a canonical official solution.

The optional worksheet and qualitative self-review exist only in the current browser page session. Refreshing or leaving clears them. Engineering Foundry does not persist worksheet text, collect official submissions, judge work, promise prizes, name winners, or generate rankings. A copied solution summary is controlled and shared by the visitor.

Discord discussion is optional. Participants should share their own work, explain assumptions, critique ideas rather than people, avoid plagiarism and proprietary interview or employer material, remove confidential information, and give constructive feedback.

## Current Community Hub and recognition

The Community Hub routes visitors to public learning, practice, career, and discussion surfaces. `1,000+ community members` refers to membership in the existing Discord community. It does not mean 1,000 active users, monthly active users, people online, contributors, challenge participants, or product accounts.

The `/leaderboard` route is a Recognition Preview. It intentionally contains no public ranks, scores, participants, winners, streaks, points, or fabricated activity. Potential contribution dimensions are described without a scoring formula.

## Future authenticated phase

A later phase may support authenticated submissions, moderation, judged community rounds, evidence-backed recognition, and an opt-in leaderboard. Before launch, those systems need clear consent and visibility controls, data retention and correction rules, eligibility and judging criteria, conflicts and appeals handling, abuse reporting, reviewer accountability, and reliable measurement. Recognition must be derived from actual recorded activity under transparent rules rather than seeded data or hidden popularity scoring.
